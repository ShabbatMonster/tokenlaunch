This does not make an AGENCY coin. AGENCY's registry only lists coins launched through agencypad.fun itself, and their site says so outright: a coin made elsewhere that merely links to an AGENCY address is not an AGENCY coin. Their launch transaction is no secret — four instructions on public programs: pump's CreateV2, then CreateFeeSharingConfig and UpdateFeeShares on pump's own fee-sharing program, then the dev buy. It is reproducible. It just would not help: 2,346 coins on chain carry that exact arrangement and AGENCY lists 200, and their site answers “not launched on AGENCY” for the rest even though the on-chain config is byte-identical bar the mint and the payer. What makes a coin theirs is a row in their database, written when you launch on their site.

What this pad does give you: a real pump.fun launch with every option below, the model and prompt recorded permanently in the token's metadata, and the CREATOR FEE RECIPIENT field above if you want the fees going to a treasury of your own rather than your launch wallet. Uses the token above (name, ticker, image, links) and the pump.fun options
        (dev buy, pair, fees). On ARM the image and metadata are pinned and the launch is built, simulated and signed against a durable
        nonce, once per ticked sender — hours ahead is fine, nonce copies never expire. Each copy opens with an on-chain guard
        (Lighthouse: “slot ≥ target”) so it cannot land before the target. Bundles go out from the bundle lead (an early
        bundle fails the guard and is dropped, free); plain sends from the plain lead — one of those reaching a leader a slot early
        lands failed and pays its fee — a pre-signed backup set on a second nonce then fires instantly. The plain lead can be
        fractional: in tests a lead of 2 landed a slot late and 3 a slot early, so 2.5 sits between. Slots run ~216ms now, so the clock counts slots,
        not seconds. Keep this tab open.

Pick the quote (custom mint for one StonkFun hasn’t admitted yet),
        set the token up above, then queue it. The image and metadata are pinned and the launch + dev buy are built and signed
        now, before the quote’s config exists. While armed it’s sent as a bundle every few hundred ms — a bundle
        that fails is dropped and costs nothing — so the first one the block engine tries after Raydium’s approval lands,
        ideally in that same block. The moment the config appears (websocket + polling) the same signed tx is blasted down every
        path. The priority fee is the total SOL, spread over the CU limit; the bribe is a tip transfer (Helius, Nozomi and Falcon want ≥ 0.001).
        With durable nonce fan-out (a one-time ~0.0015 SOL account, refundable) the launch is signed once per ticked sender, each copy
        with that sender’s tip. They share the nonce, so the first to land kills the rest: every route raced, one tip and one fee paid,
        nothing to re-sign. Sender API keys stay in this browser. Falcon is http-only, so it works from the local site, not the https one.
        A dev buy needs the quote already in the wallet. Keep this tab open — the queue survives a reload but your key doesn’t,
        so re-arm after one. RE-ARM uses the fee settings entered now.
